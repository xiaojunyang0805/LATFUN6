# LATFUN6 v0.2 power budget

> Generated by `hardware/latfun6/tools/power_budget.py` (2026-11-20). Rerun after any load or regulator change. Status: **estimate**. The FPGA fabric (dynamic VCC) and DDR3L figures are engineering estimates. Replace them with Lattice Power Calculator results at Gate A2, and with shunt measurements (RS1–RS3) on the first boards.

## Result in one paragraph

- **A typical Linux lab draws ≈ 5.2 W** from 12 V. It runs a LiteX SoC with DDR3L, Gigabit Ethernet linked, HDMI 720p, microSD, a PCIe Gen2 link and a 1 G optical SFP.
- **The worst case on the 45F is ≈ 11.4 W**: 80 % fabric at 100 MHz, all 4 SERDES lanes at 5 Gb/s, a class-II (1.5 W) SFP, plus user loads.
- **The worst case on the 85F is ≈ 13.6 W.**
- An **x1 low-profile card is limited to 10 W**, with at most 0.5 A (6 W) from 12 V. So the typical lab fits with only ~13 % margin on the 12 V pins, and the worst case does not fit at all.
- **USB-C bench power needs a 5 V / 3 A source.** Even the typical lab draws 0.94 A at 5 V, which is more than a PC USB port supplies.

## Scenarios

- **T (typical):** LiteX Linux SoC (~21 % LUT @ 75 MHz), DDR3L at 300 MT/s, GbE 1000BASE-T linked, HDMI 720p, SD reads, PCIe Gen2 lane active, SFP 1.25 G optical (0.8 W), SMA and spare lane in standby, no user loads.
- **W (worst, 45F):** 80 % LUT @ 100 MHz, VCC static doubled for hot/fast silicon, 4 lanes at 5 Gb/s, SFP power class II (1.5 W, e.g. 1000BASE-T copper SFP), SD writes, 150 mA PMOD allowance, 100 mA USB host VBUS allowance.
- **W85:** as W, with the 85F's static current (212 mA typ → 450 mA hot) and fabric scaled 84k/44k LUT: core 2.75 A, 5V_SYS 12.2 W, **12 V 13.6 W (1.13 A)**.

Conversion assumptions:
- 3V3 buck: η 0.90.
- 1V2 / 1V35 / 1V8 bucks: η 0.82–0.86.
- 1V2_SER: LDO from **1V8** (not from 3V3; this saves ≈ 0.45 W at W).
- 2V5: LDO from 3V3.
- 12 V → 5 V: LMR33630 at η 0.90.
- TPS2121 mux: 56 mΩ.

## Power-entry recommendation

- **Recommended: x4 low-profile edge (lane 0 wired) + optional 12 V DC jack for the bench.** The 25 W slot budget covers every scenario including W85, at 13.6 W, which is 54 % of the limit. The supply stays simple: 12 V only, with no 3.3 V split. The jack gives a clean bench supply independent of USB, and is ORed with the slot 12 V through a second TPS2121, which handles up to 22 V. Cost ≈ +$2 and 14 mm more finger length. The board then no longer fits closed-end x1 slots.
- **Alternative: stay x1 low-profile at 10 W.** This adds the 3.3 V split above (≈ +$1.5). Typical stays at ≈ 5.0 W, 50 % of the limit. Slot mode then needs a documented envelope of ≤ 8 W total, which excludes class-II/copper SFPs, USB host VBUS, more than 50 mA of PMOD loads and very full fabric designs. Heavy designs and the 85F become bench-only.
- Either way: **USB-C #1 needs a 5 V / 3 A adapter** for anything beyond light designs. A PC port is enough for programming a light design only. *Optional:* route the CC-pin voltage to the FPGA through a comparator, so gateware can detect the source's current capability.
